# SYSTEM ARCHIVE — Evolution Playbook

Version: 1.0  
Status: Proposed operating upgrade  
Branch: `system-archive/evolution-sprint-01`

## 1. Editorial identity

**Channel promise:** SYSTEM ARCHIVE turns surprising moments in perception into short, testable explanations of how the brain constructs our experience of reality.

The viewer should leave each video with one clear reaction: "I experienced that, and now I understand why."

### Editorial mix
- **60% Visual perception and illusions:** visual tests, attention traps, depth, color, motion and context.
- **25% Brain mechanisms:** attention, memory, language, decision-making and multisensory integration.
- **15% Time and reality:** subjective time, prediction, uncertainty and the limits of perception.

### Recurring series
1. **Göz Yanılması Laboratuvarı** — a visual challenge first, explanation second.
2. **Beynin Arka Odası** — a simple experiment revealing a cognitive mechanism.
3. **Gerçeklik Dosyası** — a bigger question about time, memory or perception, developed in long-form.

Keep the existing visual identity:
- Archive Black: `#090B10`
- Slate Panel: `#121722`
- Signal Cyan: `#73E5F4`
- Reveal Amber: `#FFB84A`
- Archive White: `#F2F4F7`
- Alert Red: `#FF4D5A` (rarely)

## 2. Six-week publishing system

Maintain the established production ambition, but make every format serve the same editorial story.

| Weekly slot | Format | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Short: visual test | Stop the scroll with participation |
| 2 | Short: explanation | Deliver the scientific reveal |
| 3 | Short: connected question or myth check | Invite comments and create a return path |
| Every 2 weeks | Long-form, 6–9 min | Turn the strongest short-form topic into a deeper episode |
| Weekly | Community post | Poll, prediction or follow-up question |

Each long-form episode should be supported by at least two related Shorts. Link those Shorts to the relevant playlist or long-form episode when available. Build playlists around the three recurring series rather than one-off topics.

### Season 01: proposed six-week slate

| Week | Anchor topic | Short 1: hook/test | Short 2: explanation | Short 3: follow-up | Long-form |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stroop effect | "Kelimeyi değil, rengini söyle" | Why the task creates interference | A harder second round | The automatic reading system |
| 2 | Change blindness | "Bu sahnede ne değişti?" | Why scene changes can go unnoticed | Ask viewers to spot a second change | How much of a scene do we actually retain? |
| 3 | Inattentional blindness | "Sayımı yaparken bunu gördün mü?" | Attention has a limited focus | A new attention challenge | The invisible things we miss |
| 4 | McGurk effect | "Gözün kulağını etkileyebilir mi?" | Visual speech changes heard speech | Audio-only versus audio-plus-video | How the senses combine |
| 5 | Müller-Lyer and context | "Hangi çizgi daha uzun?" | Context can bias visual length judgments | Change the surrounding arrows | Why seeing is not measuring |
| 6 | Time perception | "Bir saniye neden bazen uzar?" | Set up a carefully sourced demonstration | Ask when time feels fastest | The brain's subjective clock |

**Research gate:** Week 6 remains a topic placeholder until a specific phenomenon, suitable demonstration and primary source are selected. Do not present a catchy explanation as settled science without checking it.

## 3. Short-form story architecture

Target a 25–35 second first cut. Shorter is acceptable when the idea resolves cleanly.

1. **0–1.5 s: interrupt.** Show the experiment immediately. No logo animation or greeting.
2. **1.5–5 s: give one instruction.** Make the viewer participate.
3. **5–12 s: reveal.** Show the answer or the unexpected result.
4. **12–24 s: explain one mechanism.** Use plain Turkish and one idea only.
5. **24–32 s: invite a response.** Ask a specific question; make the final visual connect naturally to the opening when possible.

Do not stretch an idea to hit a duration. Remove any sentence that does not advance the test, reveal or explanation.

## 4. Long-form architecture

For a 6–9 minute episode:
- 0:00–0:20 — cold open with the strongest demonstration.
- 0:20–1:00 — the question and why it matters.
- 1:00–3:00 — reproduce the effect with clear visuals.
- 3:00–5:30 — explain the mechanism and the limits of the explanation.
- 5:30–7:30 — show a second example, boundary case or common misconception.
- Final section — one-sentence takeaway and a direct invitation to the next connected episode.

Use chapter markers when the episode has distinct sections. Do not pad runtime.

## 5. Packaging standards

### Titles
- Promise one understandable question or experience.
- Prefer concrete language over abstract scientific labels.
- Keep the scientific term in the title only when it helps discovery.
- Never claim that an illusion proves the brain is broken, that perception is always false, or that one experiment explains all people.

### Thumbnails
- One focal object or visual contradiction.
- One short text fragment, normally 2–4 words.
- Strong contrast using the existing palette.
- The thumbnail should add information instead of repeating the title.
- Avoid tiny scientific diagrams and busy collages.

### Description and metadata
- First two lines: the viewer's question and what the video demonstrates.
- Add a short source note for scientific claims.
- Use a small number of precise Turkish terms and hashtags; avoid tag stuffing.
- Add the appropriate series playlist and one closely related video.

## 6. Measurement and learning loop

Do not combine Shorts and long-form into one performance score. Compare each format against its own recent baseline and consider the traffic source and audience context.

### Shorts dashboard
- Views and engaged views.
- Stayed-to-watch versus swiped-away.
- Average view duration and average percentage viewed.
- Subscribers gained, likes and shares.
- Comments that reveal confusion, curiosity or a request for a follow-up.

### Long-form dashboard
- Impressions and click-through rate.
- Average view duration and audience-retention curve.
- Traffic sources.
- Watch time and subscribers gained.
- Returning viewers and end-screen continuation where available.

### Weekly experiment rule
Change one major creative variable at a time: opening visual, first sentence, pacing, explanation density or call to action. Record the hypothesis before publishing and the result after a comparable observation window. Do not overreact to a handful of views or a small CTR movement; inspect traffic sources and sample size before drawing a conclusion.

## 7. Production quality gate

A video is **READY FOR OWNER REVIEW** only when all are true:
- The central scientific claim has a primary research paper or a reputable scientific source.
- The script distinguishes observation, interpretation and uncertainty.
- The demonstration actually supports the claim being made.
- On-screen text, narration and captions agree.
- Turkish spelling, pronunciation and subtitle timing are checked.
- Audio is intelligible on a phone speaker and the first frame works without sound.
- The final render has been watched from start to finish on a mobile-sized screen.
- Title, thumbnail, description, source note and playlist are included.

Never mark a draft APPROVED or PUBLISHED without the channel owner's explicit decision.

## 8. Ready-to-produce Short: Stroop effect

**Working title:** Kelimeyi Okuma, Rengini Söyle!  
**Series:** Beynin Arka Odası  
**Target first cut:** 28–32 seconds

### Script
**0–2 s — Visual:** Show the word "MAVİ" printed in orange.  
**Voice:** "Kelimeyi okuma. Sadece yazının rengini söyle."

**2–6 s — Visual:** Show three incongruent color-word cards with enough time to answer.  
**Voice:** "Hazır mısın? Bir, iki, üç!"

**6–11 s — Visual:** Freeze the cards and reveal the ink colors.  
**Voice:** "Zorlandın mı? Çünkü kelimeyi okumak çok alışılmış bir işlem."

**11–23 s — Visual:** Highlight the written word and ink color separately.  
**Voice:** "Kelimenin anlamı ile mürekkebin rengi çakıştığında, renk adını söylemek yavaşlayabilir. Bu etkiye Stroop etkisi denir."

**23–31 s — Visual:** Return to the first card.  
**Voice:** "İkinci turda daha hızlı olabilir misin? Kaçını doğru yaptığını yorumlara yaz."

### On-screen copy
- "KELİMEYİ DEĞİL, RENGİ SÖYLE"
- "MAVİ" (orange ink)
- "STROOP ETKİSİ"
- "İKİNCİ TUR?"

### Packaging
- **Title alternative A:** Kelimeyi Okuma, Rengini Söyle!
- **Title alternative B:** Beynin Bu Testte Neden Takılıyor?
- **Thumbnail text:** "RENK Mİ, KELİME Mİ?"
- **Pinned question:** "İlk turda kaç rengi doğru söyledin?"
- **Description opening:** "Bu kısa testte kelimeyi okumadan yalnızca mürekkebin rengini söyle. Kelimenin anlamı ile yazı rengi çakıştığında tepkin neden yavaşlayabilir? Stroop etkisini birlikte deneyelim."

### Scientific source
- J. R. Stroop (1935), "Studies of interference in serial verbal reactions", *Journal of Experimental Psychology*, 18(6), 643–662. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0054651
- The original study describes interference when a color word and its ink color conflict. Keep the narration focused on slower color naming under conflicting conditions; do not claim that every person experiences an identical effect.

## 10. Six-week success criteria

The first season is an experiment, not a promise of a particular view or subscriber count.

At the end of six weeks, review:
- Whether all planned episodes passed the science and mobile QC gates.
- Which opening patterns improved stayed-to-watch on Shorts.
- Which explanations produced stronger average percentage viewed.
- Which long-form topics held attention beyond the opening.
- Which videos generated subscribers, thoughtful comments and follow-on viewing.
- Whether one of the three recurring series clearly outperformed the others.

Promote only evidence-backed winning patterns into Season 02. Retire or redesign formats that repeatedly fail to earn attention or deliver a clear viewer payoff.
