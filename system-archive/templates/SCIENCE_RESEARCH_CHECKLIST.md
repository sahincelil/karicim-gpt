# Science Research Checklist

Use this before writing, during script review and again before release.

## Source hierarchy
1. Original peer-reviewed research for what was actually tested.
2. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses for the wider evidence base.
3. Reputable academic or scientific institutions for accessible explanations.
4. General media only as a lead to better sources, not as the sole support for a central claim.

## Verify each source
- [ ] Opened the paper or authoritative record, not just a search snippet.
- [ ] Title, authors, year, journal and DOI match.
- [ ] Study design and sample are understood well enough to describe.
- [ ] The result is not overstated beyond the measured outcome.
- [ ] Correlation, causation, hypothesis and interpretation are distinguished.
- [ ] Limitations, replications or material disagreements are checked.
- [ ] The spoken claim matches the source, not a popular paraphrase.
- [ ] A direct source link is saved in the episode brief and description notes.

## Claim wording scale
- **Observed in this study:** use when describing the specific experiment.
- **Evidence suggests:** use when a body of research supports a cautious conclusion.
- **One possible explanation:** use for a theory or mechanism that is not uniquely established.
- **Not established:** use when the experiment cannot answer the proposed broader claim.

Avoid:
- "Scientists proved that the brain always..."
- "Everyone sees..."
- "Your brain is broken..."
- "This one experiment explains..."
- "The brain tricks you on purpose..."

Prefer:
- "In this task, participants were slower when..."
- "This effect can make it harder to..."
- "The result suggests..."
- "Researchers use this phenomenon to study..."

## Demonstration integrity
- [ ] Correct stimuli and instructions are used.
- [ ] The edit does not predetermine the answer deceptively.
- [ ] Viewer participation time is sufficient.
- [ ] Text, color, sound and motion remain legible on a phone.
- [ ] The demonstration does not imply diagnosis or a medical test.
- [ ] If the demonstration is illustrative rather than an exact replication, label it as an illustration.

## Rights and integrity
- [ ] Media is original, properly licensed, public domain or used with a documented permission basis.
- [ ] Do not recreate copyrighted figures or video clips without permission.
- [ ] No fabricated citations, synthetic participant results or false expert endorsements.
