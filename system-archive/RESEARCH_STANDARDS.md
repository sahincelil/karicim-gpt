# Research and fact-check standards

Before scripting a factual science video:
1. Write the exact claim the video intends to make.
2. Find the closest primary source (peer-reviewed paper, review, university, or research institution publication).
3. Check whether the result is replicated, contested, limited to a specific population, or only demonstrated in a lab.
4. Separate observation from interpretation.
5. Avoid turning correlations into causes.
6. Avoid precise numbers unless the source and context support them.
7. Keep a source note with author / organization, title, year, and URL.
8. Re-check the final narration against the source, not just the headline or abstract.

## Preferred source order
- Peer-reviewed systematic reviews and meta-analyses where appropriate
- Original peer-reviewed research for specific experiments
- Reputable universities, scientific societies, and research institutions
- High-quality science journalism as context, not sole support for a strong claim

## Red flags
- “Scientists proved” without a specific source
- Universal claims based on one small study
- Brain-region explanations implying one region performs a complex behavior alone
- Exact millisecond claims without a precise, relevant source
- Medical or therapeutic claims outside the evidence

Use “may”, “can”, “in this experiment”, or “research suggests” when they better reflect the evidence.
